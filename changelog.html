2026-09-10 · v0.7.3 · Deep dive: Aryeo, Pixieset and the photographer stack

Why: Aryeo is the model for a niche all-in-one and Pixieset is the gallery product most photographers already pay for. We needed the mechanics of both, what people love and hate, and which other tools creatives pay for that we should absorb, integrate with, or ignore.

Added

  • research/Deep Dive Aryeo, Pixieset and the Photographer Stack.md: Aryeo's data model (orders and listings feeding the order form, scheduler with drive time, pay-before-download delivery, property sites and the agent portal), the Zillow relationship and its terms, Pixieset's gallery detail (access modes, favourites, download PIN, expiry, sizes, store) and its weaker studio manager, the love and hate lists with sources, and a verdict on thirty other tools: absorb, integrate, or ignore.

For the team

  • Aryeo's completeness comes from one data model feeding six surfaces; copy the model and the flow, not the 2014-looking form. Halation cannot replicate Zillow Showcase, so offer one-click export to Aryeo Lite and sell independence with a revocable licence.
  • Pixieset wins on the gallery itself and loses on trust: failed uploads, weak support, no bulk export, instant closure on cancel, and a "sum of the parts" bill. Copy the gallery detail; beat the studio manager by making gallery events automation triggers.

2026-09-10 · v0.7.3 · Deep dive: HoneyBook and Monday

Why: the business side of Halation replaces both. We needed the exact mechanics that make them work, what people love and hate, and what to copy, beat or skip.

Added

  • research/Deep Dive HoneyBook and Monday.md: HoneyBook's single client page (pick services, sign, pay, book, and that completion drives the pipeline and automations) and Monday's status pill that drives lanes, dashboards, automations and My Work, each explained so an engineer could build it, with the love and hate lists, tables and open questions.

For the team

  • Both lose customers over money, not features: HoneyBook's card and ACH fees, dispute holds and the 2025 price rise; Monday's seat minimums, automation meters and gated features. Halation's cleanest wins: a quiet client page reached by a magic link, money shown gross, fee and net with a visible payout date, no seat minimums or automation meters, free client and contractor access, and opt-in AI that never writes to a record without a click.

2026-09-10 · v0.7.3 · Deep dive: Frame.io and Dropbox

Why: Halation has to replace both. We needed to know exactly which behaviours make them what they are, what people love and hate, and what to copy, beat or skip.

Added

  • research/Deep Dive Frame.io and Dropbox.md: the defining features of each explained so an engineer could build them, the love and hate lists with sources, must copy / must beat / can skip tables, and the open questions (sync client versus mounted drive, which comment exports ship first, what counts toward quota, importers before Frame.io's December 2026 API cut-off).

For the team

  • The short version: Frame.io's moat is a handful of small behaviours (typing pauses the player, a comment pins to a frame or a range, drawings never touch the file, dropping v2 on v1 makes a stack, clients need only a name and email). Both products' most hated behaviour is storage lock-outs and surprise billing, which is our opening: never lock, go read-only with a grace period, publish a per-TB price, one-click cancel.

2026-09-09 · v0.7.2 · The mark, revised after the logo research

Why: the logo research came back an hour after the first mark shipped, and it found two real problems with the frame-and-corner-light: its silhouette (rounded square, dot top right) is Instagram's glyph, which matters for a photo and video audience, and its outline greyed out at favicon size. Better to change it now than after the team has used it.

Changed

  • The mark is now a capital H whose crossbar is the light: a solid orange disc where the bar would be, biting into both uprights, with a thin ring of ground around it. Same idea, the light eats the edge, and it teaches the name. No gradient and no glow in the static mark; the bloom is reserved for motion in three places (app launch, the landing hero, the Paid moment).
  • The app draws the same H at 22 px in the landing nav and the setup wizard. The favicon and iOS icon are the H.
  • On light backgrounds the disc uses a deeper orange so it holds contrast on paper.
  • brand/ has the new kit: the mark for dark, light, one colour and small sizes, the lockup, PNGs at every size, an Apple touch icon and a 400 px avatar. The retired frame mark is kept in brand/alternates/.

Added

  • Research: research/Logo Practices for SaaS.md. What holds up in SaaS marks, the system to ship around one, colour and type conventions, 2024 to 2026 trends to avoid, five directions for Halation with the H recommended, and a clearance checklist. First report in the new research shape, and the first one posted to the research channel.

For the team

  • Look at the top-left of the landing page and at the favicon in the tab. Does it read as an H at a glance? If it does not for most people, the frame mark is the fallback.
  • Name clearance flag: two creative studios already trade as "Halation" (an art-direction and photography studio, and an arts-branding agency) and the handle @halationco is taken. Not fatal, but it needs an attorney's read before anything public.
